\section{Conclusion}

This thesis developed a reproducible data pipeline and linked-view dashboard for comparing London Fire Brigade incident demand and first-pump response performance. The dashboard brings borough maps, monthly trends, response-time distributions, ranked values, recorded denominators, and data limitations into one interface.

\subsection{Answers to the Research Questions}

The linked views let a user move from a borough-level colour pattern to its monthly trace, response-time distribution, and exact tabular values. Recorded denominators, confidence intervals, small-sample warnings, and the partial-month marker remain visible during that comparison.

\subsection{Contribution Statement}

The project has four main outputs. First, it provides a reproducible open-data pipeline for LFB incident records, borough summaries, mobilisation joins, footprint centroids, and historical station-address proximity, with sidecar dictionaries and data-quality memos. Second, it implements a Flask/D3 dashboard for borough-level demand and response-performance comparison. Third, it separates assignment footprints from postcode-geocoded historical addresses and states the additional inputs needed for travel-time analysis. Fourth, it supplies a task-based evaluation protocol and a compact think-aloud summary of how three participants used the dashboard.

\subsection{Future Work}

Future work should begin with a reproducible evaluation package: retained anonymised session records, supported tasks, and a larger sample. Technical priorities are direct demand ranking, the deferred filters, a fuller response distribution, and point-level uncertainty encodings. Travel-time analysis would additionally need verified current station locations, a road network, capacity and availability data, and operational validation.

\subsection{Lessons Learned}

Separating the data pipeline, API, interface, and tests made it possible to revise presentation and wording without changing the underlying calculations. More importantly, denominators and limitations need to appear beside a comparison rather than in a distant note. Provenance sidecars, figures, and tests also make the reported numbers easier to trace when the source data or interpretation changes.
